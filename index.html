01 / The Platform
01
Fiscal Strength

Alternative Revenue & Forestry

What We Need to Do:

Expand non-tax municipal revenue streams to fund legacy capital investments—specifically the pool renovation/replacement and expanded community clean-up—without placing the financial burden on local homeowners.

How I Will Deliver:

  • Maximize the Alberni Valley Community Forest tenure and partner with BC Timber Sales (BCTS) to increase timber revenue and annual dividends back to City Hall.
  • Aggressively pursue provincial and federal capital grants to ensure outside funding carries the majority of major recreational upgrades.
  • Review underutilized city-owned parcels for long-term commercial lease opportunities that expand our non-residential tax base.
02
Core Infrastructure

Fixing The Basics

What We Need to Do:

Refocus municipal spending on essential services and core physical infrastructure to protect our existing assets before taking on speculative new commitments.

How I Will Deliver:

  • Implement a public, multi-year Asset Management Schedule showing clear timelines for priority road repaving, sewer main replacements, and storm drainage fixes.
  • Target pedestrian safety investments on high-traffic neighbourhood corridors, school routes, and the Argyle/Uptown connector.
  • Work collaboratively with community caretakers, including the Alberni Pacific Railway, to support industrial and heritage assets sustainably.
03
Safer Streets

Clean & Safe Neighbourhoods

What We Need to Do:

Ensure every resident and business owner feels safe and proud of their street through rapid municipal upkeep and balanced public safety.

How I Will Deliver:

  • Establish rapid-response cleanup and routine maintenance schedules for parks, downtown alleys, and commercial corridors.
  • Align municipal bylaw enforcement directly with the local RCMP detachment's operational priorities to coordinate resources efficiently.
  • Implement Crime Prevention Through Environmental Design (CPTED) standards to improve sight-lines, public lighting, and accessibility across key community spaces.
04
Community Well-Being

Youth Opportunity & Senior Living

What We Need to Do:

Invest upstream in our youth to prevent crises before they start, while giving our seniors accessible, affordable options to stay in their community.

How I Will Deliver:

  • Coordinate with regional service tables, schools, and non-profits to expand after-school sports, recreation, and early-intervention youth programming.
  • Streamline municipal zoning and development approvals for accessible ground-floor ranchers, patio homes, and universal-design apartments tailored for aging in place.
  • Facilitate partnerships between non-profit housing providers and regional health agencies to secure land and permits for youth-focused shelter and support facilities.
05
Local Economy

Commercial Vibrancy & Small Business

What We Need to Do:

Create a predictable, business-friendly environment that fills empty storefronts and keeps local dollars circulating in our economy.

How I Will Deliver:

  • Cut red tape with a streamlined municipal permitting path for commercial renovations, storefront signage, and outdoor seating.
  • Execute a targeted vacancy recruitment strategy along 3rd Avenue and Argyle Street to attract small enterprises and creative trades.
  • Prioritize local suppliers, catering, and service vendors for municipal contracts and city-run events.
06
Active Community

Parks & Community Recreation

What We Need to Do:

Restore and maintain our public recreation spaces so local families and sports leagues have dependable, well-kept facilities.

How I Will Deliver:

  • Partner directly with local sports user groups to conduct an immediate condition audit on our fields, tackling turf drainage and regular upkeep.
  • Improve trail access, signage, and maintenance across local waterfronts and recreational green spaces.
  • Add practical, low-cost park amenities—including shaded seating, accessible playground equipment, and clean public washrooms—in residential neighbourhoods.
02 / About Sam

Sam Atwal

Candidate for Council

Executive leadership rooted in natural resources, community service, and local collaboration.

I’ve always believed that real leadership starts with showing up, listening with an open mind, and earning trust through honest work. As a husband, father, and community advocate, I’m running for Port Alberni City Council because I care deeply about the future of the place my family calls home. Like so many of you, I want our town to be a place where our streets feel safe, our kids have positive opportunities to stay out of trouble, our seniors have accessible options to age comfortably in place, and our local businesses can genuinely thrive. During my four years serving on municipal council in Sparwood, I saw firsthand that local government isn’t just about bylaws and line-item spreadsheets—it’s about people. It means listening to differing viewpoints, making transparent decisions, and having the courage to make tough choices when they serve the long-term good of the community. Professionally, I’ve spent over a decade in senior leadership and strategic consulting across resource stewardship, forestry operations, economic development, and Indigenous partnerships. That background taught me how to break down silos, negotiate strong agreements, and bring people together to solve complex problems. At City Hall, I want to apply that experience to maximize our Community Forest partnerships with BCTS, unlock alternative revenue for major projects like the pool without overburdening residential taxpayers, and deliver a clear, data-driven plan to fix our basic infrastructure—from our roads and water mains to our ballfields and public parks. I’m not interested in politics for the sake of politics. I believe in candid conversations, disciplined fiscal oversight, and getting the basics right. I won’t always tell you what’s easiest to hear, but I will always listen, I will always be transparent, and I will always work hard to do what’s right for Port Alberni. Because leadership isn't about taking the easy road—it’s about rolling up our sleeves and doing the work.
